What E.L.I does

Works in your real apps

Ask in plain English, such as "build a dashboard from my sales file". E.L.I opens the program, clicks, types and fills the fields.

Checks before it says "done"

It reads the result back. If something is wrong, it undoes it and tries again.

Offers the next step

It turns your own files into dashboards, schedules and status digests, and builds them when you say "Go for it".

Learns your routine

Show it a task once. Next time, just say "do weekly update".

Keeps data on your PC

Its AI models run locally and it collects no telemetry. Nothing goes to the cloud unless you switch that on yourself.

Works offline

A built-in offline library and knowledge graph answer many questions without the internet.

Works with every native desktop application on your PC, as well as File Explorer and web pages in your browser.

Who it is for

E.L.I is built for everyone who works on a Windows PC: students, freelancers, small-business owners, professionals and whole teams. It takes on the clicking, typing and checking in the apps you already use, from a household budget to a project schedule, and it does it without sending your data to the cloud.

You stay in control

  • Autonomy levels. You choose how much E.L.I does on its own, from suggesting only to working tasks end to end. It still asks before irreversible or outward-facing steps, such as sending, submitting, deleting or paying.
  • Live control is opt-in. E.L.I cannot use your mouse and keyboard until you turn that on for the session.
  • Stop button. One click, or Ctrl+Alt+Esc, halts any desktop job.
  • Tamper-evident log. Every action is recorded in a hash-chained audit log.
  • Cloud off by default. Prompts go to an outside AI provider only if you switch that on with your own account.

Where it is today

E.L.I is a Windows beta. It is dependable for short tasks you supervise; long multi-step jobs in complex apps are still improving.

What it found strong

  • A wide range of real desktop work, including applications E.L.I was never trained on
  • Controls built in from the start: approvals, a stop button and a tamper-evident log
  • Jobs keep running when the chat window is closed, and an interrupted job resumes from its last verified step
  • Useful offline, from a built-in library of 1.5 million articles

What is still open

  • Half of all live runs (291 of 583) carry a verified result; the others rely on E.L.I's own report
  • The separate verifiers caught three false "done" claims. All three are fixed, and they still count against a "reliable" rating
  • The latest week, mostly benchmark test jobs, completed 75% (76 of 102)
  • Open-ended requests that E.L.I must plan from scratch succeed about half the time (47%)
  • Without a graphics card, each step that reads the screen takes 38 to 68 seconds
  • No real business job has yet been defined, baselined and evaluated

The assessment was prepared for Influence IPM LLC from E.L.I's source code and run logs on one development PC. It is not a third-party audit. Clients and pilot partners can ask us for the full report.

In development: a lighter, standalone E.L.I

Alongside the desktop beta we are building a standalone prototype that runs as a small local service with a browser dashboard. It is not part of the beta and is not available for download yet.

One verified step at a time

It looks, plans, acts, checks and updates, so every action is confirmed before the next one starts.

Talk to it while it works

Ask questions or change the job mid-task. Pause, stop and an emergency stop are always available.

Approval first

Desktop access is off until you approve it for the session, and only the applications you allow are driven.

Requirements

  • Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit
  • About 20 GB of free disk space; 16 GB of memory recommended
  • Your own licences for the applications you want E.L.I to work in
  • A graphics card is optional but makes screen reading much faster
